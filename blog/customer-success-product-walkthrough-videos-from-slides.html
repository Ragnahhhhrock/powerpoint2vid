Customer Success: Product Walkthrough Videos From Slides

Customer success teams explain the same things over and over: how to set up the first project, how to invite the team, where to find reports. Each explanation is a call, an email thread or a long help article. A short product walkthrough video built from slides can explain those things once, the same way every time, and be ready whenever a customer needs it.

This playbook shows how to plan, build and share walkthrough videos from the decks and screenshots you already have.

Why a product walkthrough video helps customer success

  • Customers learn at their own pace. They can pause, rewind and come back later.
  • Fewer repeat calls. The basics are covered by video, so live calls can focus on goals and problems.
  • Consistent answers. Every customer gets the same accurate steps, no matter which CSM they have.
  • It scales with the book of business. One video serves ten customers or a thousand.
  • It helps the people you never meet. Your champion can forward it to new team members.

Why slides instead of screen recording?

Screen recordings are great for showing a live product. But they have drawbacks for repeat use:

  • Every UI change means re-recording.
  • Mistakes, pauses and mouse wandering creep in.
  • Recording your own voice takes time and a quiet room.

Slides built from annotated screenshots are easier to keep current. When a button moves, you swap one screenshot. The narration comes from speaker notes, so you edit text instead of re-recording audio.

Both have a place. Many teams use slide-based walkthroughs for stable, core workflows and quick screen recordings for one-off answers.

Step 1: Pick the walkthroughs that matter most

Start with the questions your team answers most often. Look at:

  • The first-week onboarding calls. What do you explain every time?
  • Support tickets that are really “how do I” questions.
  • Features customers pay for but don’t use.

Pick three to five topics for your first batch. Each topic gets its own short video.

Step 2: Plan one video per task

Keep each walkthrough to one task with a clear finish line. “Set up your first project” is a task. “Everything about projects” is a course.

A simple structure:

  1. Goal slide. What the viewer will be able to do by the end.
  2. Step slides. One step per slide, with a screenshot.
  3. Check slide. What it looks like when it worked.
  4. Next step slide. What to do next, and where to get help.

Step 3: Build slides from screenshots

For each step:

  • Take a clean screenshot of the relevant screen. Use demo data, never real customer data.
  • Crop it to the part that matters.
  • Add a simple highlight, such as a box or arrow, around the button or field.
  • Write a headline that says the action: “Click New project in the top right”.

Keep text large. If a screenshot is too detailed to read in video, zoom in on the relevant area.

Step 4: Write the speaker notes as the voiceover

The speaker notes become the narration. Write them for the ear: short sentences, one instruction at a time.

Sample notes for a three-step walkthrough:

Slide 1: In this video, you’ll set up your first project. It takes about two minutes.

Slide 2: Click New project in the top right corner. Give it a name your team will recognise.

Slide 3: Now choose a template. If you’re not sure, pick Standard. You can change it later.

Slide 4: That’s it. Your project appears in the left menu. Next, invite your team. There’s a short video on that too.

If your product names or acronyms are tricky for a synthetic narrator, spell them the way they should sound in the notes. Our post on pronunciation tips for text-to-speech narration has more ideas.

Step 5: Produce and check

  • Keep the slides calm and clear. Walkthroughs should feel helpful, not flashy.
  • Use a modest speaking speed so people can follow along in the product.
  • Burn in captions so customers can watch without sound.
  • Leave each screenshot on screen long enough to read, with enough narration to cover it.
  • Watch the whole thing once as if you were a new customer.

Step 6: Put the videos where customers look

Place How to use it
Onboarding emails One video per email, matched to the step the customer is on
Help centre Embed the video at the top of the matching article
In-app help Link to the video from the relevant screen
Follow-up after calls Send the walkthrough that matches what you discussed
Customer portal or community Keep a library organised by task

Always pair the video with written steps, so people can scan or copy them.

Step 7: Keep them current

Set a review rhythm, such as checking every walkthrough after each major release. Because the videos are built from slides, updating means:

  1. Swap the changed screenshot.
  2. Edit the speaker note if the step changed.
  3. Create the video again and replace the old file.

Keep the source decks in a shared folder with clear names, so anyone on the team can update them.

Quick checklist

  • One task per video, with a clear finish line.
  • Goal, steps, check, next step.
  • Clean screenshots with demo data only.
  • Headlines that say the action.
  • Speaker notes written for the ear.
  • Captions on, modest speaking speed.
  • Written steps alongside every video.
  • A review after each release.
