Explainer Videos for Internal Communication

Every organisation has messages that need to reach everyone the same way: a new leave process, a system change, a restructure, a reminder about security. An internal communication video lets you explain it once, clearly, and send that same explanation to every person who needs it, whenever they’re ready to watch.

This post covers when a short video beats an email or a meeting, what makes one work, and how to build it from a deck you probably already have.

What an internal communication video is good for

Not every message needs a video. A one-line reminder belongs in an email. But video earns its place when a message:

  • Needs explaining, not just announcing. “We’re changing how expense claims work” needs steps and reasons.
  • Goes to many people at different times. Shift workers, part-timers and staff in other time zones can’t all attend one briefing.
  • Must be consistent. When managers each brief their own teams, the message drifts. A video says the same thing every time.
  • Will be asked about again. New starters next month will have the same questions. A video answers them too.

Why narrated slides suit internal messages

Research on multimedia learning, such as Richard Mayer’s work, suggests people learn better from words and pictures together than from words alone. A narrated slide video gives people both: the key point on screen and a plain spoken explanation alongside it.

There are practical benefits too:

  • Async by default. People watch when it suits them. No calendar wrangling.
  • Accessible. Captions help people who are deaf or hard of hearing, people reading in a second language, and anyone watching in an open-plan office with the sound off.
  • Easy to update. When the policy changes, edit the slide and create the video again. No reshoot.
  • No camera needed. Plenty of good communicators would rather not appear on video. Slides and a clear voice do the job.

Email, meeting or video?

Here’s a simple way to choose.

Message type Best format Why
Short reminder or deadline Email or chat Quick to read, nothing to explain
Sensitive news (redundancies, major change) Live conversation first People need to ask questions and be heard
New process or system Video plus written steps Needs showing, and people will come back to it
Quarterly update Short video Consistent, watchable at any time
Policy change Video plus the policy document Video explains, document is the reference

A video should never replace a human conversation for news that affects people’s jobs or wellbeing. Use it to support those conversations, for example as a follow-up that explains the practical details.

What makes an internal video work

Keep it short

Aim for the shortest video that does the job. Several focused videos are better than one long one. If you are covering three changes, consider three short videos.

Lead with what changes for the viewer

Staff want to know: what’s different, when, and what do I need to do? Put that up front.

Before: “Update on the finance system transformation program” After: “From 1 March, submit expense claims in the new app”

One idea per slide

Each slide should carry one step or one point. If a slide has a dense paragraph, split it or move the detail into the speaker notes, where it becomes narration.

End with a clear action

Tell people exactly what to do and where to get help. “Submit your first claim in the new app by 15 March. Questions go to the finance helpdesk.”

A sample script

Here’s how the speaker notes for a three-slide internal update might read:

Slide 1: From the first of March, expense claims move to the new finance app. The old form will stop working on that date.

Slide 2: To submit a claim, open the app, take a photo of your receipt, and choose the cost centre. It takes about a minute.

Slide 3: Please submit your first claim in the new app by the fifteenth of March. If you get stuck, contact the finance helpdesk. The link is in the email with this video.

Short sentences. Dates said the way people say them. One action at the end.

Getting staff to actually watch

  • Send it where people already look. Your intranet, team chat or the weekly email.
  • Say how long it is. “A two-minute video on the new expense process” gets more clicks than “Please watch”.
  • Put the key point in the message too. Some people won’t press play. Make sure they still get the headline.
  • Ask managers to share it. A note from a team lead carries weight.

If you’re producing internal training as well as updates, our post on compliance and policy training videos covers that side in more depth.

Check your policy for internal content

Internal decks often contain sensitive information: unreleased changes, internal figures, staff details. Before you use any online tool, check your organisation’s policy on using online tools with that kind of content.

Key takeaways

  • Use video for messages that need explaining, reach many people, or will be asked about again.
  • Lead with what changes for the viewer and when.
  • Keep videos short, one idea per slide, with one clear action at the end.
  • Add captions so everyone can follow.
  • Pair sensitive news with a live conversation, not a video alone.
