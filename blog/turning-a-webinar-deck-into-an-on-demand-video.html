Turning a Webinar Deck into an On-Demand Video

You ran the webinar. People registered, some showed up, and plenty didn’t. The deck is sitting in a folder, and the recording is an hour of “can everyone see my screen?” Turning your webinar slides to video gives you a second, tighter asset: a short on-demand version that explains the same ideas without the waiting, the polls or the dropped audio.

This guide shows how to cut a live deck down, rewrite it for narration and publish something people will actually finish.

Why turn webinar slides to video instead of posting the recording

A webinar recording is a record of an event. An on-demand video is a piece of content. They do different jobs.

The recording has housekeeping at the start, questions that only made sense live, and long pauses while people type in the chat. Viewers who arrive later skip around, get lost and leave. A purpose-built video starts with the point, moves at a steady pace and ends with one clear next step.

Keep the recording if registrants asked for it. But don’t make it the only thing you publish.

Step 1: Cut the deck back to the core story

Open a copy of the webinar deck and delete everything that belonged to the live event:

  • Welcome and housekeeping slides (“Mute your mic”, “Questions in the chat”)
  • Speaker bio slides longer than one line
  • Poll slides and “Any questions?” slides
  • Agenda slides for a 60-minute session
  • Repeated summary slides you added to give late joiners a catch-up

What’s left is usually the real content. Now arrange it in a simple order: situation, problem, answer, proof, next step. If two slides make the same point, keep the stronger one.

A good target for an on-demand version is a few minutes, not an hour. If the webinar covered three big ideas, consider making three short videos instead of one long one.

Step 2: Rewrite headlines so each slide makes a point

Live presenters can rescue a vague slide by talking over it. A video can’t lean on that as much. Every slide headline should say the takeaway.

Before: “Q3 campaign results”

After: “Search ads drove most of our new trial sign-ups in Q3”

Before: “Our approach”

After: “We test one landing page change at a time”

If a headline won’t fit on one or two lines, the slide is probably doing two jobs. Split it.

Step 3: Turn what you said live into speaker notes

The narration in your on-demand video comes from your speaker notes. If you ad-libbed the webinar, the notes may be empty or full of shorthand. This is where the real work is.

Use the webinar recording or transcript as raw material. For each slide, pull out what you actually said, then tidy it for the ear:

  • Short sentences, one idea each
  • Spoken words, not written ones (“about a third” rather than “approx. 33%”)
  • No references to the live event (“as Sarah mentioned in the chat”)
  • Two to five sentences per slide is plenty for most slides

Here’s a sample note for the “Search ads” slide above:

Most of our new trial sign-ups last quarter came from search ads. Social brought in plenty of clicks, but fewer of those people signed up. So for the next quarter, we’re moving more budget into search, and testing social with a smaller spend.

For more on this, see how to write speaker notes that become a great voiceover.

Step 4: Replace the live Q&A with an answers slide

The questions your audience asked live are gold. They tell you exactly where people get stuck. Don’t throw them away.

Pick the two or three questions that came up most, and give each one a slide with the question as the headline and a short answer in the notes. For example:

  • “Do I need a big budget to start?”
  • “How long before we see results?”
  • “What if we don’t have a designer?”

This turns your on-demand video into a better resource than the live session, because every viewer gets the answers without having to ask.

Step 5: Choose length, style and settings

Decide on a length before you convert. A rough guide by use:

Where it will live Suggested approach
Landing page or email One short video on the main idea
Resource library A short series, one video per topic
Social feed One idea, captions on, strong first slide
Sales follow-up The core story plus the answers slide

For more detail, read how long should an explainer video be?

When you convert, a few settings matter for webinar content:

  • Narration: your speaker notes become the voice, so the rewritten script is what viewers hear. Choose a voice and speaking speed that suit your audience.
  • Captions: keep them on. Many people watch on-demand content with the sound off at first.
  • Pacing: make sure title and section slides with little or no narration still stay on screen long enough to read.
  • Tone: a calm, clean look suits most business webinars. A punchier, faster cut works for social.

Step 6: Give it a home and a next step

An on-demand video needs a clear job. Decide what you want a viewer to do when it ends: book a call, start a trial, download a guide. Put that on the last slide as a single, specific action, and say it in the notes.

Then publish it where people already look. That might be the webinar registration page (updated to say “Watch on demand”), your resource library, a follow-up email to everyone who registered, or a YouTube channel.

Quick checklist

  • Housekeeping, polls and bio slides removed
  • Every headline states a takeaway
  • Speaker notes rewritten for the ear, with no live-event references
  • Top questions answered on their own slides
  • Captions on, pacing checked on slides with little narration
  • One clear call to action on the final slide
  • Video placed where registrants and new visitors will find it
