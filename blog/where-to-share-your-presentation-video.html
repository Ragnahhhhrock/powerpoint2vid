Where to Share Your Presentation Video

You have turned your deck into a video. Now it needs an audience. Where you share presentation video files decides who sees them, how they find them and whether they watch to the end. A training video, a pitch and a lecture each belong in different places, and the same video often needs small changes for each one.

This guide covers the main places to share a presentation video, what each is good for, and how to prepare the file before you upload.

Before you share: get the file ready

A little preparation saves trouble on every platform.

  • Check the format. PowerPoint2Vid gives you an MP4 to download, with WebM also available. MP4 is accepted almost everywhere; if a platform prefers another format, common conversion tools can change it. For more, see MP4 or WebM? Choosing a Format for Your Presentation Video.
  • Name the file clearly. “q4-onboarding-it-setup.mp4” is easier to manage than “export-final-v3.mp4”.
  • Make the first slide count. Many platforms use an early frame as the thumbnail. A title slide with a clear headline works better than a logo.
  • Burn in captions. Many people watch with the sound off. Captions burned into the video show on any platform.
  • Write a short description. One or two sentences on what the video covers and who it is for. You will reuse it everywhere.

Where to share presentation video files

Your own website

Best for: product explainers, how-it-works videos, pricing walkthroughs.

Your website is where people decide whether to buy, sign up or get in touch. A video placed beside the content it explains can answer questions at the right moment. Host it on a video platform and embed it, or use your site’s own video player, and check the page still loads quickly. For placement ideas, see Where to Put an Explainer Video on Your Website.

YouTube

Best for: public tutorials, explainers you want people to find through search, course previews.

YouTube is a search engine as well as a video site. A clear title, a useful description and a relevant thumbnail help people find your video. You can upload publicly, or set a video as unlisted so only people with the link can see it. YouTube also lets you upload caption files, which is useful if you want viewers to be able to switch captions on and off.

LinkedIn

Best for: thought leadership, product updates, sales follow-ups, event promotion.

LinkedIn supports native video uploads in posts. Keep videos short and make sure the first few seconds carry the point, because people scroll quickly. Captions matter here, as many people browse at work with the sound off. Write a post that gives the takeaway in the first line, then let the video add the detail.

Your LMS

Best for: lecture videos, flipped classroom material, compliance and onboarding modules.

Learning platforms such as Moodle, Canvas and Blackboard let you add video to course pages, either by uploading or by embedding from your institution’s video service. Put each video next to the activity it supports, and tell students how long it is. Check your institution’s guidance on file size and hosting.

Email

Best for: sales follow-ups, client updates, short internal announcements.

Most email clients do not play video inline, and large attachments are often blocked. Share a link instead. A thumbnail image that links to the video page works well. Say the length in the subject or first line so people know it is a small ask.

Subject: 3-minute walkthrough of the new rostering process

Hi team, here’s a short video showing how to request shifts in the new system. Watch it before Monday’s switch-over. Questions to me.

Internal tools

Best for: training, policy updates, all-hands summaries, process changes.

Shared drives, intranets and team chat tools are good places for internal videos. Use your organisation’s approved storage for anything confidential. Pin important videos to the relevant channel or page so new staff can find them later.

Social media

Best for: teasers, launches, event promos, quick tips.

Short, bold videos suit social feeds. Make a cut-down version of your deck with fewer slides and shorter headlines. A punchier cut with bold headlines can help it stand out. Captions are essential.

Matching the channel to the goal

Goal Best channels Video length
Be found in search YouTube, your website As long as the topic needs
Convert visitors Your website Short
Teach a course LMS Short, one concept each
Follow up a sales call Email link, unlisted video Short
Train staff LMS, intranet, shared drive Short modules
Build awareness LinkedIn, social media Very short

One deck, several versions

You rarely need a brand new video for each channel. Duplicate the deck and adjust:

  • Full version for your website, YouTube or LMS
  • Short version with three to five slides for LinkedIn and social
  • Personalised version with the client’s name on the first slide for sales

Each takes minutes, because the speaker notes do most of the work.

Quick checklist

  • Check the file format suits the platform
  • Name the file clearly
  • Use a strong title slide as the first frame
  • Burn in captions for silent viewing
  • Write a one- or two-sentence description
  • Choose channels based on your goal
  • Make short versions for social feeds
  • Share links, not attachments, by email
  • Keep confidential videos in approved internal storage
