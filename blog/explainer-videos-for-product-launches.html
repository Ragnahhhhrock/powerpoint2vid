Explainer Videos for Product Launches

A product launch video gives every visitor, customer and journalist the same clear explanation of what you built and why it matters. You don’t need a film crew to make one. If you have a launch deck, you already have most of the material. This post covers why a launch video is worth making, what it should say, and how to build one quickly from slides.

Why a product launch video earns its place

Launch week is noisy. People skim announcements, open emails on their phones and decide in seconds whether to look closer. A short video helps in a few practical ways:

  • It explains once, for everyone. Your sales team, support staff, partners and customers all hear the same story, in the same words.
  • It works without you in the room. A prospect can watch it at 10pm. A partner can forward it to their boss.
  • It travels. The same file can sit on the product page, in the launch email, on social channels and in a press kit.
  • It combines words and pictures. Research on multimedia learning, such as Richard Mayer’s work, suggests people understand better from narration and visuals together than from text alone. A launch is exactly when you want people to understand quickly.

For a small team, the biggest benefit is reuse. You write the explanation carefully once, and it keeps working long after launch day.

What a launch video should say

Keep it simple. Most good launch videos follow the same five beats.

  1. The problem. One sentence the viewer recognises from their own work.
  2. The product. What it is, in plain words.
  3. How it works. Two or three steps, shown on screen.
  4. The proof. A customer result, a demo moment or a concrete detail.
  5. The next step. Where to go and what to do.

A sample script

Here is a speaker-note script for a fictional invoicing app, one paragraph per slide.

Slide 1: Chasing late invoices eats your Friday afternoons.

Slide 2: Ledgerly sends reminders for you, on a schedule you set.

Slide 3: Connect your accounting tool, choose your reminder rhythm, and Ledgerly does the rest.

Slide 4: Every reminder links straight to a payment page, so clients can pay in two taps.

Slide 5: Start a trial at ledgerly dot com and set up your first reminder in five minutes.

Notice the short sentences. They are written for the ear, not the page, so they sound natural when read aloud as narration.

How long should it be?

Shorter than you think. A launch video’s job is to make someone want the next step, not to document every feature. Aim for:

  • Product page and email: around one to two minutes.
  • Social clips: a short cut of one or two slides, captioned for silent viewing.
  • Sales or partner briefings: a longer version can go deeper, but keep it focused on what that audience needs.

If your deck has thirty slides, your launch video probably needs six to ten. Cut anything that is “nice to know”.

Building a launch video from your deck

You likely already have a launch deck for internal briefings or investors. Turn it into a viewer-ready version.

Step 1: Copy and trim the deck

Duplicate the file and remove internal slides: pricing debates, roadmaps, team org charts. Keep only what a customer needs.

Step 2: Rewrite headlines as claims

Swap topic labels for statements.

  • Before: “Reminders”
  • After: “Reminders go out on your schedule, not when you remember”

Step 3: Write the narration into speaker notes

One short paragraph per slide, matching the five beats above. Read it aloud once to catch awkward phrasing.

Step 4: Show the product

Use real screenshots, cropped tight to the feature you are describing. A single clear screenshot beats a collage.

Step 5: Keep the look on brand

A launch often suits a polished, confident look. Use your brand colours and fonts in the deck so the finished scenes feel like your product, whether your brand is quiet and refined or bold and punchy.

Step 6: Add captions

Many people watch launch posts with the sound off. Captions mean the message still lands.

Where to use your launch video

Placement Purpose Tip
Product page, near the top Explain the product to new visitors Place it beside the headline and main call to action
Launch email Lift interest in the announcement Link a thumbnail to the video page rather than attaching the file
Social channels Reach people who don’t know you yet Use captions and a short cut
Press or partner kit Give others an accurate summary Include the full version and a one-line description
Sales follow-ups Let reps send a clear explanation Pair it with a short personal note

Keep it current after launch

Products change. Screens get redesigned, prices move, features are added. A video filmed with actors and a studio is expensive to update. A video built from slides is not: you edit the slide, update the speaker note and create the video again.

Build this into your launch plan:

  • Keep the source deck in a shared folder with a clear name.
  • Note which slides contain screenshots that will date.
  • Review the video at each major release.

Key takeaways

  • A product launch video explains your product the same way to everyone, at any hour.
  • Follow five beats: problem, product, how it works, proof, next step.
  • Keep it short and focused on what a customer needs.
  • Build it from your existing launch deck, with claims as headlines and narration in the notes.
  • Add captions for silent viewing.
  • Plan to update it, and keep the source deck handy.
