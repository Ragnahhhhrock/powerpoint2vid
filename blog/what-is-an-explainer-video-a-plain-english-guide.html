What Is an Explainer Video? A Plain-English Guide

What is an explainer video? It’s a short video that explains one thing clearly: how a product works, why a policy changed, how to complete a task or what a piece of research found. This guide covers what makes a video an explainer, the common types, where they’re used and how to make one without a studio.

What is an explainer video, exactly?

An explainer video has three features.

  1. One subject. It answers a single question or explains a single idea.
  2. A clear structure. It moves from the problem or question to the answer, usually in a few minutes.
  3. Words and pictures together. Narration explains while visuals show.

It isn’t an advertisement, though it can support marketing. It isn’t a recorded meeting, though it can replace one. The goal is understanding.

Common types of explainer video

Explainers come in a few familiar forms. They can all do the same job; the difference is how they’re made.

Type What it looks like Typical effort
Animated illustration Custom characters and scenes High: scripting, illustration, animation
Live action People on camera High: filming, lighting, sound
Screen recording A software walkthrough Low to medium
Talking head A person speaking to camera Medium
Narrated slides A deck with voice and motion Low: you start from slides you have

Narrated slides are often overlooked. Yet many organisations already explain their most important ideas in slide decks. Turning those decks into video is the shortest route to an explainer.

Where explainer videos are used

Marketing and sales

A product explainer on a website answers “what does this do?” before someone has to ask. A narrated pitch deck lets a prospect understand the offer when the salesperson isn’t there.

Education

Lecturers use short explainers for flipped-classroom content, revision and topics students regularly find hard.

Training and onboarding

HR and learning teams use them so every new hire hears the same accurate explanation of policies and processes.

Internal communication

A short video update can replace a long email nobody reads in full.

Research and public information

Researchers and nonprofits use explainers to share findings and causes with audiences outside their field.

What makes an explainer work

It answers one question

“How do I submit an expense claim?” is a good explainer topic. “Everything about finance” isn’t.

It follows a simple story

A reliable shape is situation, problem, answer, proof, next step.

Here’s that shape as a short script for a returns process:

  • Situation: “You’ve bought something from us and it isn’t right.”
  • Problem: “You want your money back without a long wait.”
  • Answer: “Start a return from your order page. It takes about two minutes.”
  • Proof: “Most refunds are processed within five business days of the item arriving.” (Use your own real figure here.)
  • Next step: “Go to your orders now and choose Return.”

The words and pictures match

When the narration says “start from your order page”, the screen should show the order page. Research on multimedia learning, such as Richard Mayer’s work, suggests people learn better from words and pictures together than from words alone, especially when the two line up.

It’s short enough to finish

There’s no perfect length, but every extra minute should earn its place. If it’s getting long, it may be two explainers. A quick test: if you can’t sum up the video in one sentence, it’s probably trying to cover too much. Split it, give each part its own title, and link them together.

It’s accessible

Captions help people who are deaf or hard of hearing, people watching in a second language and anyone watching with the sound off. Good contrast and readable text matter too.

Why teams make them

  • Explain once, reuse many times. The same video answers the same question for every new customer, student or hire.
  • Async by default. People watch when it suits them.
  • Consistency. Everyone gets the same message.
  • Reach. Video works on websites, learning management systems, YouTube, LinkedIn and email.

How to make an explainer video from slides

If you already have a deck on the topic, you have most of an explainer.

  1. Trim the deck to the slides that answer one question.
  2. Give each slide one idea and a headline that states it.
  3. Write the narration in the speaker notes, in short spoken sentences.
  4. Convert it with a tool that narrates the notes and animates the slides.
  5. Preview, adjust and download.

For the step-by-step process, see How to Convert PowerPoint to Video: The Complete Guide. If captions are on your list, Captions on Presentation Videos: Why and How covers your options.

Key takeaways

  • An explainer video explains one thing clearly, with words and pictures together.
  • Animation and live action are common, but narrated slides do the same job with far less effort.
  • A simple story structure and matching visuals do most of the work.
  • Captions and readable slides make it work for more people.
