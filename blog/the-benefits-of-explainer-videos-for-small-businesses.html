The Benefits of Explainer Videos for Small Businesses

Small businesses explain the same things over and over: what the product does, how pricing works, how to get started. The benefits of explainer videos come down to this: you explain it well once, and the video keeps doing the job. This post covers where explainers help a small team most, and how to make one without an agency budget.

The benefits of explainer videos, one by one

1. Explain once, reuse many times

Every small business has questions it answers daily. “How does delivery work?” “What’s included in the basic plan?” “How do I book?” A short video answers each one the same way for every customer, without anyone on your team stopping what they’re doing.

2. Clearer than text alone

Some ideas are hard to get across in a paragraph. A narrated video can show the steps while explaining them. Research on multimedia learning, such as Richard Mayer’s work, suggests people learn better from words and pictures together than from words alone.

3. People watch when it suits them

Prospects browse your site at night and on weekends. A video on the product page works when you’re offline. A narrated pitch deck in a follow-up email explains your offer before the next call.

4. Every customer hears the same message

When different staff explain the same thing, details drift. A video keeps the message accurate and on brand.

5. Fewer back-and-forth emails

If the same question keeps landing in your inbox, a video link in the reply, or on the FAQ page, can answer it fully the first time.

6. It works in many places

One video can go on your homepage, a product page, YouTube, LinkedIn, a newsletter and a sales email. You can also cut versions for different channels from the same deck.

7. It helps more people understand

Captions help customers who are deaf or hard of hearing, people reading in a second language and anyone scrolling with the sound off.

Where to use an explainer first

Pick the spot where a clear explanation would change what people do.

Situation Explainer idea
Visitors don’t understand what you sell “What we do in 90 seconds” on the homepage
Customers ask the same setup question “Getting started” video in the welcome email
Prospects stall after the first call Narrated pitch deck sent as a follow-up
You’re launching a new product or service Launch explainer for the website and social
Staff explain the same process to new hires Onboarding walkthrough

A simple script structure

A short explainer usually follows the same shape: situation, problem, answer, proof, next step. Here’s an example for a local bookkeeping business.

  • Situation: “You run a small business and do your own books.”
  • Problem: “Every quarter, your BAS takes a whole weekend, and you’re never sure it’s right.”
  • Answer: “We handle your bookkeeping and BAS each quarter, using the software you already have.”
  • Proof: “Here’s what a typical month looks like for one of our clients.” (Show a real, approved example.)
  • Next step: “Book a fifteen-minute call from our website.”

That’s five slides. With a sentence or two of narration each, it’s a video of about a minute. For more on structure, see How to Structure a Presentation: A Simple Story Framework.

How to make one on a small budget

Agencies can produce high-end animated explainers, and for some campaigns that’s worth it. But many small businesses already have the raw material: a pitch deck, a sales presentation or a webinar deck.

  1. Pick one question the video will answer.
  2. Trim your deck to the slides that answer it.
  3. Rewrite headlines so each one states a point.
  4. Write the narration in the speaker notes, in short spoken sentences.
  5. Convert the deck into a narrated video.
  6. Preview it with the sound on and off, then export.

With PowerPoint2Vid, the narration comes from your speaker notes, and you can choose a voice and speaking speed. The deck is upgraded rather than just recorded: stronger story, animated scenes, polished transitions and captions. If the deck includes pricing or plans you haven’t announced, check your organisation’s policy on online tools before you upload it.

Be clear about what you’re getting: a produced explainer built from your own slides and notes, not a filmed shoot with presenters. For most “here’s how this works” explainers, that’s all you need.

Making the video work harder

  • Put it where the question comes up. On the pricing page, not buried on an “about” page.
  • Keep it short. Cover one question. Make another video for the next one.
  • Turn on captions. Many people will watch with the sound off.
  • Update it when things change. Edit the deck and export again.
  • Track what you can. Look at plays, page time and whether people take the next step.

Key takeaways

  • Explainer videos answer common questions once, consistently and around the clock.
  • Start where a clear explanation would change behaviour: homepage, onboarding or sales follow-up.
  • A simple five-part script keeps it short and focused.
  • A deck you already have is the cheapest starting point.
