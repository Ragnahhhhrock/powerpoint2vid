MP4 or WebM? Choosing a Format for Your Presentation Video

When you turn a deck into a video, the last question is often the most confusing one: what file format should it be? Most people search for PowerPoint to MP4 because MP4 is the format they’ve heard of. But depending on your tools, you may end up with a WebM file instead. Both are perfectly good. The trick is knowing where each one plays and what to do when a platform asks for the other.

This guide explains the two formats in plain terms and helps you pick the right one for where your video is going.

What a video format actually is

A video file has two layers:

  • The container. This is the file type, like .mp4 or .webm. It wraps up the video, the audio and some information about them.
  • The codecs. These are the methods used to compress the video and audio inside the container.

You don’t need to know codec names to make good choices. What matters is whether the place you’re sending the video can play it.

MP4 in brief

MP4 is the most widely recognised video format. It plays on almost every phone, computer, smart TV and video platform. Most video editors, LMSs and social platforms accept it. When a form says “upload your video”, MP4 is the safest bet.

Good for:

  • Uploading to platforms that list specific formats
  • Sending to people who’ll open it on any device
  • Playing in desktop media players
  • Importing into most video editors

WebM in brief

WebM is an open video format designed for the web. It’s widely supported in modern browsers, including Chrome, Edge and Firefox, and many video editors and platforms accept it too.

Good for:

  • Embedding on your own website
  • Playing in a modern browser
  • Uploading to platforms that accept it
  • Archiving or editing in tools that support it

Comparing the two

Question MP4 WebM
Plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox Yes Yes
Plays on most phones and TVs Yes Varies by device and app
Accepted by video platforms Almost always Often, check the platform
Accepted by LMSs Usually Sometimes, check your system
Opens in most video editors Yes Many, not all

The short version: MP4 is the most portable. WebM is a solid web format that sometimes needs converting for a specific destination.

PowerPoint to MP4: your options

There are a few routes from a deck to a video file.

PowerPoint’s own export

Desktop PowerPoint can save a presentation as a video, and MP4 is one of the formats it offers. It uses your slide timings and any narration you’ve recorded yourself. If you’re happy recording your own voice and your deck already has its timings set, it’s a sensible option.

Converting WebM to MP4

If you have a WebM file and need MP4, conversion is straightforward. Common options include:

  • Desktop video converters and media players. Many popular tools can open a WebM and save or export it as MP4.
  • Video editors. Import the WebM and export as MP4.
  • Command-line tools. If you’re comfortable with them, widely used open-source tools can convert between the two in one line.

Whatever you use, play the converted file all the way through once to check the audio stayed in sync.

Which format should you choose?

Work backwards from where the video is going.

  1. Your own website. WebM or MP4 both work in modern browsers. Many sites offer both.
  2. YouTube or similar platforms. Check the platform’s list of supported formats. Many accept WebM. MP4 is accepted almost everywhere.
  3. Your LMS. Check what your institution’s system accepts. If in doubt, convert to MP4.
  4. Email or a shared drive for colleagues. MP4 is safest, since people will open it on all sorts of devices.
  5. Social media. Check each platform’s upload guidance. MP4 is the usual safe choice.
  6. Further editing. Check your editor’s supported formats, or convert to MP4 first.

Example: one video, three destinations

Say you’ve made a five-minute product overview from your sales deck and have it as a WebM file.

  • Website: Embed the WebM directly on the product page. It plays in modern browsers.
  • YouTube: Upload the WebM if the platform accepts it, or convert to MP4 first.
  • LMS for customer training: The system’s upload page lists MP4 only, so you convert once and upload the MP4.

One export, one quick conversion, three places covered.

Tips for a clean export

Whatever format you end up with, these habits help the file come out right:

  • Check the narration first. Fix mispronunciations and timing in your notes before you export, so you only export once.
  • Confirm the audio is there. Play the file in a different player to make sure the narration came through.
  • Watch the final file all the way through. Check the audio, the last slide and the ending.

Quick checklist

  • Know where the video is going before you export
  • Check the destination’s accepted formats
  • Use MP4 when you are unsure, and WebM for the web if it’s accepted
  • Convert between them for platforms or devices that need it
  • Play the final file through once to check audio and video

From PowerPoint to MP4 or WebM

The format question matters less than it seems. Both formats are widely supported, and converting between them is routine. What matters more is a clear deck, a good script and a video that tells the story well.
