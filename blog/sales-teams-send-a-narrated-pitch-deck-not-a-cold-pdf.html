Sales Teams: Send a Narrated Pitch Deck, Not a Cold PDF

You had a good call. The prospect asked for “something to share with the team”. So you sent the deck as a PDF. Now it sits in an inbox, forwarded to people who never heard your pitch, and the slides that made sense with you talking over them look thin on their own. A video pitch deck fixes that. It is the same slides, with your explanation attached, so the deck makes the case when you are not there to make it.

This playbook walks through how to turn the deck you already use into a short narrated video that gets watched, not skimmed.

Why a PDF loses the deal for you

Sales slides are built to be presented. They carry headlines, a chart, maybe a logo wall. The reasoning lives in your voice. Strip out the voice and three things happen:

  • The story breaks. Readers jump to the pricing slide first and judge it without context.
  • The champion has to retell it. Your contact now has to pitch your product to their boss, from memory, using your slides.
  • Nobody sees the emphasis. A PDF gives every slide the same weight. You know which slide matters most. The reader does not.

A narrated video keeps the order, the emphasis and the explanation together. Your champion can forward one link, and everyone who opens it hears the same pitch.

What a good video pitch deck looks like

Keep it short and specific to the buyer. Aim for a handful of slides and a few minutes of narration. A tight structure helps:

  1. Their situation. What you heard on the call, in their words.
  2. The problem. What it is costing them, described plainly.
  3. The answer. How your product solves that problem. One slide, not five.
  4. Proof. A customer story, a result, or a short demo screenshot.
  5. Next step. One clear ask, with a date if you can.

This is the situation, problem, answer, proof, next step pattern. It works because it mirrors how a buyer thinks about a purchase.

Write headlines that carry the argument

If someone watches with the sound off, the headlines should still tell the story. Compare:

  • Before: “Our Platform”
  • After: “Your team gets one place to track every renewal”

The second headline makes a claim the viewer can agree or disagree with. For more on this, see Slide Headlines That Make a Point.

Step by step: from sales deck to narrated video

1. Duplicate and trim the deck

Never edit your master deck for one prospect. Make a copy, then cut. Delete the company history slide, the full feature list and anything the buyer did not ask about. If a slide needs two headlines, split it or drop half.

2. Personalise the first and last slides

Put the prospect’s company name and the problem they described on slide one. Put the agreed next step on the last slide. Those two slides do most of the work of making a generic deck feel written for them.

3. Write the speaker notes as a script

The speaker notes become the narration, so write them the way you would say them. Short sentences. Plain words. Numbers spoken the way people say them.

Here is a sample script for a proof slide:

“Here’s a team that looked a lot like yours. They were chasing renewals across three spreadsheets. After they moved to one shared view, their account managers stopped asking each other who owned what. That’s the change we’d expect for you too.”

Read it aloud once. If you trip over a phrase, the text-to-speech voice probably will too.

4. Choose a style that suits your brand

PowerPoint2Vid has six video styles. For sales, two tend to fit well:

  • Clean keeps your slides full-bleed with shapes fading up. It looks like your deck, just moving.
  • Keynote launch puts each slide on a rounded card on a dark stage with a slow drifting camera. It feels like a product announcement.

Pick one, preview it with the voice, and adjust the speaking speed until it sounds natural.

5. Turn on captions

Many people open a forwarded video at their desk with the sound off. Burned-in captions show the narration sentence by sentence, so the pitch still lands without audio.

6. Export and send

Export the video and host it wherever your team already shares files, or upload it to your video platform of choice. Then send a short email with the link and one line of context.

How to send it so it gets watched

The email matters as much as the video. Keep it brief:

Subject: The 3-minute version for your team

Hi Jordan, as promised, here’s a short narrated walkthrough of what we discussed. It covers the renewal tracking problem and how we’d fix it. The last slide has the next step we agreed on. Happy to answer questions on Thursday’s call.

A few habits help:

  • Put the length in the subject line. “3-minute” tells people it is a small ask.
  • Say what is in it. One sentence on the problem it covers.
  • Point to the next step. The video should support a conversation, not replace it.

This is async selling at its best. If you want the wider case for it, read Async Video vs Live Meetings: When a Video Saves Everyone Time.

What a narrated deck will not do

Be honest about the limits. A slide video is not a live demo, and it does not replace your relationship with the buyer. PowerPoint2Vid narrates with your browser’s built-in voices rather than your own, so the video sounds polished and consistent but not personal. If a deal depends on your face and voice, record a short personal intro separately and send both. If you prefer to narrate yourself, PowerPoint’s own video export can record your voice and timings as you present.

Keep your deck private

Sales decks often hold pricing, customer names and roadmap details. PowerPoint2Vid runs entirely in your browser. The .pptx is parsed, animated, narrated and recorded on your own device, and your slides are never uploaded. That makes it a sensible choice for confidential material.

Quick checklist

  • Duplicate the master deck, then trim to the slides this buyer needs
  • Name the prospect and their problem on slide one
  • End on one clear next step
  • Write headlines that make a claim
  • Write speaker notes as a spoken script
  • Preview with the voice and adjust speaking speed
  • Turn on burned-in captions for silent viewing
  • Send with a short email that states the length and the ask
