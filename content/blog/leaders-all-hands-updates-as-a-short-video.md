All-hands meetings are meant to keep everyone on the same page. In practice, people miss them for shifts, leave, time zones and back-to-back meetings. The ones who attend remember a slide or two. An all-hands video update gives every employee the same clear message, at a time that works for them, built from the deck you were going to present anyway.

This playbook is for leaders, and the HR, L&D and internal comms people who support them.

## Why turn the all-hands into a video

A short video doesn't replace every live meeting. It does solve some common problems.

- **Everyone gets the same message.** Shift workers, part-timers and remote staff hear exactly what head office heard.
- **Async by default.** People watch when it suits them, so there's no need to schedule around every team.
- **Shorter live sessions.** If the update is in a video, the live meeting can focus on questions and discussion.
- **A record to come back to.** New starters can watch last quarter's update to understand where the business is heading.
- **Accuracy.** Sensitive announcements, like a restructure or policy change, are worded carefully once, not paraphrased by every manager.

## What belongs in a short all-hands video update

Keep it focused. A few minutes is usually plenty. A simple structure:

1. **Where we are:** one or two headline results
2. **What changed:** the most important news since the last update
3. **What's next:** priorities for the coming period
4. **What it means for you:** practical impact on teams
5. **What to do:** one clear action or where to find more

Leave detailed financials, team-by-team reports and long Q&A for the live session or written follow-up.

## Step 1: Strip the deck down

Most all-hands decks grow over time. Every department adds a slide. Start with a copy and cut hard.

- Keep slides that matter to everyone
- Move team-specific detail into separate team videos or written updates
- Merge slides that say the same thing
- Remove anything that only makes sense with live discussion

## Step 2: Write headlines that tell the story

Viewers should get the message from the headlines alone.

**Before:** "Q4 Results"

**After:** "We finished the year ahead of plan on customer retention"

**Before:** "New Systems"

**After:** "The new rostering app goes live for all sites on 3 March"

**Before:** "People Update"

**After:** "We're hiring 12 roles in the first half of the year"

Use only figures you can stand behind. If a number is still being finalised, say so in the notes, or leave it out until it's confirmed.

## Step 3: Write the script in your own voice

The narration comes from your speaker notes, read aloud by a synthetic voice. That means the words still need to sound like you, even if the voice isn't.

- Write the way you speak to your team
- Short sentences, plain words
- Explain any acronym the first time
- Be direct about difficult news

A sample note for the rostering slide:

> The new rostering app goes live at every site on the third of March. You'll be able to see your shifts, swap them and request leave from your phone. Your manager will run a short training session in the week before launch. If you have questions before then, ask your manager or contact the HR team.

That note covers what's happening, when, why it helps and what to do.

## Step 4: Handle sensitive news carefully

Some updates are hard: restructures, budget cuts, leadership changes. A video can help here because the wording is considered and consistent. But it shouldn't be the only channel.

- Tell directly affected people first, in person or by phone
- Use the video to share the same message with everyone else
- Say clearly where people can ask questions or get support
- Follow up with a live session or manager conversations

## Step 5: Choose a style, voice and captions

For all-hands content, keep the look straightforward so attention stays on the message. A calmer, more cinematic feel suits an annual vision or strategy update.

Choose a voice and speaking speed that sounds clear and steady. Turn on captions: many staff will watch on their phones in noisy break rooms or with the sound off.

## Step 6: Share it where people will see it

Decide how staff will find the video:

- Link it in the all-staff email or newsletter
- Post it on your intranet or internal messaging channel
- Ask managers to play it at team huddles for frontline staff
- Add it to the onboarding pack for new starters

Pair it with a way to respond: a question form, a channel or the next live Q&A session. For more on internal video, see [explainer videos for internal communication](/blog/explainer-videos-for-internal-communication).

## Check your policy first

All-hands decks often include confidential results and plans. Before you upload a deck to any online tool, check your organisation's policy on using online tools for this kind of content. Once the video is ready, share it through your usual internal channels with the right access settings.

## Quick checklist

- Deck cut to what matters to everyone
- Headlines tell the story on their own
- Notes written in your voice, with clear actions
- Sensitive news handled in person first
- Captions on, clear voice chosen
- Shared through the channels staff actually use
- A way for people to ask questions
