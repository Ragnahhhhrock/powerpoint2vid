The deck is nearly done. The content is right, the story holds together, and you're ready to send it or present it. This is exactly when small mistakes slip through: a typo in a headline, a chart with last year's numbers, a slide that's two shades off the brand colour. A short presentation checklist for the final pass catches them before your audience does.

Work through the sections below in order. Most decks take fifteen to thirty minutes.

## Pass 1: Story and structure

Start with the big picture. Switch to slide sorter view, or zoom out so you can see every slide at once.

- **Does the deck follow a clear arc?** Situation, problem, answer, proof, next step.
- **Does every slide earn its place?** If you removed it, would anyone notice? If not, cut it.
- **Is there one idea per slide?** A slide that needs two headlines is two slides.
- **Does it end with one clear next step?** Not "Questions?" but a specific action.

### The headline test

Read only the slide headlines, top to bottom. They should tell the whole story on their own.

**Before:**
- Background
- Data
- Options
- Recommendation

**After:**
- Customer wait times doubled over winter
- Most delays happen at the first transfer
- Three ways to remove that transfer
- Route calls by topic from the first menu

If your headlines read like the "before" list, rewrite them before doing anything else.

## Pass 2: Words

Now read every slide properly.

- **Spelling and grammar.** Run the spell checker, then read slowly anyway. Spell checkers miss correctly spelt wrong words.
- **Consistent spelling.** Pick one style (colour, organise, centre) and stick to it.
- **Names and titles.** Check every person's name, role and organisation, especially the client's.
- **Numbers.** Check every figure against the source. Make sure totals add up and dates are current.
- **Trim text.** Cut words that don't add meaning. Move detail into speaker notes.

## Pass 3: Design consistency

Small inconsistencies make a deck feel less trustworthy, even if viewers can't say why.

- **Fonts:** the same typefaces and sizes for the same jobs throughout
- **Colours:** brand colours used consistently, no near-matches
- **Alignment:** titles in the same position on every slide, items lined up to a grid
- **Spacing:** margins consistent, nothing crammed against an edge
- **Images:** sharp, not stretched, not pixelated
- **Charts:** same style across the deck, one message each, direct labels

Flick through the deck quickly in presentation mode. Anything that jumps or shifts between slides is worth fixing.

A useful habit: if several people built the deck, check that every slide uses a layout from the same slide master. Slides pasted in from older decks often carry their own fonts and colours, and resetting them to the master layout fixes most inconsistencies in one step.

## Pass 4: Accessibility

A few checks make your deck usable by more people.

- **Text size:** body text 24pt or larger if the deck will be watched on screen or as a video
- **Contrast:** aim for WCAG AA, which means at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text
- **Colour alone:** never the only way to show meaning; add labels, patterns or icons
- **Alt text:** add short descriptions to meaningful images and charts
- **Reading order:** check that slide elements read in a sensible order for screen readers
- **Slide titles:** every slide has a unique title, even if it's hidden

PowerPoint includes an accessibility checker that flags many of these issues. Run it, and fix what it finds.

## Pass 5: Speaker notes

Notes are easy to forget, but they matter, especially if the deck will be presented by someone else or turned into a narrated video.

- Every content slide has notes
- Notes match the current slide, not an earlier draft
- Sentences are short and written for speaking
- No internal comments left in ("check with legal", "fix this chart")

Here's a quick before and after:

**Before:** "wait times – see chart – mention Q3 issue?? – TBC"

**After:** "Wait times doubled over winter. The chart shows the jump starting in June. Most of that came from calls being transferred more than once."

## Pass 6: File and sharing details

The final checks are about the file itself.

- **File name:** clear and versioned, such as `service-review-v3-final.pptx`
- **File size:** compress large images if the file is too big to email
- **Hidden content:** remove hidden slides, comments and personal information you don't want to share
- **Links:** click every link and check it works
- **Format:** decide whether to share the .pptx, a PDF, a video, or a combination

## The whole presentation checklist in one place

| Area | Key check |
|---|---|
| Story | Headlines alone tell the story |
| Words | Names, numbers and spelling verified |
| Design | Fonts, colours and alignment consistent |
| Accessibility | Text size, contrast, alt text, checker run |
| Notes | Complete, current and written for speaking |
| File | Named, sized, cleaned and links tested |
