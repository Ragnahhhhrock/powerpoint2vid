Most decks get presented once, then disappear into a shared drive. That is a lot of thinking left on the shelf. If you repurpose a presentation into video, the same slides can become a website explainer, a set of social clips, a sales follow-up and an onboarding module. Each one reaches a different audience, and none of them starts from a blank page.

This post shows how to plan one deck for several videos, and how to adapt it for each use without rebuilding everything.

## Why it pays to repurpose a presentation into video

You already did the hard part. The deck has a story, a structure, approved messaging and visuals that match your brand. Creating each video from scratch means repeating that work and risking mixed messages.

Repurposing gives you:

- **Consistency.** Every video says the same thing, in the same words, with the same visuals.
- **Speed.** You edit slides and notes, not storyboards and footage.
- **Reach.** Different formats suit different channels and different moments in a buyer's journey.
- **Easier updates.** When a price, feature or date changes, fix it in the master deck and re-export the versions that use it.

## Map the deck before you cut it

Start by listing every slide and tagging it with what it does. A typical product deck might look like this:

| Slide | Job | Website explainer | Social clip | Sales follow-up | Onboarding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The problem | Hook | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Our answer | Main message | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| How it works (3 slides) | Detail | Yes | One slide | Yes | Yes |
| Customer story | Proof | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Pricing | Decision | No | No | Yes | No |
| Getting started (4 slides) | How-to | No | No | No | Yes |
| Call to action | Next step | Yes | Yes | Tailored | Tailored |

Now you can see which slides each video needs. Save a copy of the deck for each version and delete what does not belong.

## Four videos from one deck

### 1. The website explainer

The full story, trimmed to essentials. Aim for one to two minutes. Open with the problem, show the answer, prove it, and end with a single call to action. For guidance on length, see [How Long Should an Explainer Video Be?](/blog/how-long-should-an-explainer-video-be)

### 2. Short social clips

Pull single ideas into very short videos of three to five slides. Each clip should make sense on its own, without the rest of the deck. Social viewers often watch with the sound off, so burned-in captions matter here. A bold, high-energy look can help a clip stand out in a feed.

### 3. The sales follow-up

After a call, send a narrated version tailored to that prospect's question. Keep the pricing slide, add a slide that addresses their specific concern, and end with a clear next step such as booking a demo.

### 4. The onboarding version

Drop the persuasion and keep the how-to. New customers need "how it works" and "getting started", not "why you should buy". A calmer, friendlier look suits this better than a launch-style one.

## Rewrite the notes for each audience

The slides may stay the same, but the narration should not. A prospect and a new customer need different words.

Here is one slide, "Reports in one click", with notes for two versions.

**Website explainer:**

"Building a monthly report used to take an afternoon. Now it takes one click. Pick your date range, press export, and the report is ready to send."

**Onboarding:**

"To build your first report, open the Reports tab and choose a date range. Then press Export. The report downloads straight away, ready to share with your team."

The same visual, a different job. For more on scripts, see [How to Write Speaker Notes That Become a Great Voiceover](/blog/how-to-write-speaker-notes-that-become-a-great-voiceover).

## Keep your versions organised

Repurposing goes wrong when nobody knows which file is current. A simple system helps.

- Keep one **master deck** with every slide and the approved messaging.
- Save each version as a separate file: `product-explainer-web.pptx`, `product-clip-reports.pptx`, `product-onboarding.pptx`.
- Note in each file which master slides it uses.
- When the master changes, check which versions need re-exporting.
- Name video files with the version and date.

## Match the style to the channel

Video styles change how the same slides feel. A quick guide:

- **Website hero:** A cinematic, polished feel for a strong first impression.
- **Social feed:** Bold and energetic, or warmer and handmade, depending on your brand.
- **Sales follow-up:** Clean and restrained, so the content leads.
- **Onboarding:** Friendly and product-like.

Keep the style consistent within each channel so viewers start to recognise your videos.

## Key takeaways

- One good deck can produce several videos for different audiences.
- Map each slide to the videos that need it before cutting.
- Rewrite speaker notes for each audience, even when slides stay the same.
- Keep a master deck and clearly named versions.
- Match video style and length to the channel.
