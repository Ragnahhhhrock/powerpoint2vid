Nonprofits explain things constantly. To donors, to volunteers, to grant panels, to board members and to the people they serve. Most of that explaining happens in presentations, often given by the same handful of overstretched people. A nonprofit explainer video lets you tell your story once, clearly, and share it everywhere, without a film crew or an agency budget.

This post covers where explainer videos help charities and community groups most, and how to build one from slides you already have.

## Why explainer videos suit nonprofits

### Explain once, reuse often

Every new volunteer needs the same induction. Every potential donor asks the same first questions. A short video answers those questions the same way every time, so your team can focus on the conversations that need a person.

### Consistent, accurate messaging

When five different volunteers describe your program, you get five versions. A video keeps the core message accurate, including the things that must be said correctly, like eligibility or how donations are used.

### Reach beyond the room

Fundraising events and community talks reach the people who turn up. A video reaches supporters on your website, in email newsletters, on social media and in a grant application if the funder accepts video.

### Accessibility and inclusion

Captions and narration help people who are deaf or hard of hearing, people reading in a second language and anyone watching with the sound off. For organisations serving diverse communities, that matters.

### Low cost from slides

Most nonprofits already have a presentation about their work. Turning that deck into a narrated video avoids the cost of cameras, studios and voice talent.

## Where a nonprofit explainer video helps most

| Audience | What the video should do |
|---|---|
| Potential donors | Show the problem, your approach and what a gift makes possible |
| Volunteers | Explain the role, what to expect and how to get started |
| Grant panels | Summarise the project clearly alongside the written application |
| Board and committees | Brief members on a decision or update before a meeting |
| Community members | Explain a service, who it's for and how to access it |

Pick one audience per video. A video trying to speak to donors and service users at once usually speaks clearly to neither.

## Build the story before the slides

A strong explainer follows a simple structure: situation, problem, answer, proof, next step. For a fundraising video, it might look like this:

1. **Situation:** "Many older people in our region live alone."
2. **Problem:** "Loneliness affects their health and wellbeing, and many don't know where to turn."
3. **Answer:** "Our volunteers make weekly friendly phone calls and home visits."
4. **Proof:** A real (consented and anonymised where needed) story, or a number from your own program records.
5. **Next step:** "Become a monthly supporter" or "Sign up to volunteer."

Use only figures from your own records or reliable public sources. If you're not sure a number is accurate, leave it out. Trust is your most valuable asset.

## Write the script for a listener

The narration is read from your speaker notes, so write them the way you'd talk to a supporter over a cup of tea.

**Before (written style):**

> The organisation delivers a comprehensive suite of social connection services to isolated seniors across the metropolitan area.

**After (spoken style):**

> Every week, our volunteers call and visit older people who live alone. For many of them, it's the conversation they look forward to most.

The second version is warmer, simpler and easier to follow by ear.

## Design tips for nonprofit decks

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs two headlines, make it two slides.
- **Headlines that make a point.** "Your gift funds a year of weekly calls" beats "How donations are used".
- **Real photos, used with care.** Get consent, respect dignity, and avoid images that reduce people to their circumstances.
- **Large text and strong contrast.** Body text 24pt or larger, and contrast that meets WCAG guidance.
- **A single, specific call to action.** One link, one ask.

## Choosing a tone and voice

For most nonprofit content, a calm tone works well. Serious, reflective stories suit restraint. Community and education content suits warmth. Volunteer inductions and board briefings suit a plain, clear approach.

Choose a voice and adjust the speaking speed. A slightly slower pace gives emotional moments room to land. Captions help the message come through even with the sound off.

## Handle sensitive material with care

Nonprofit decks often include sensitive material: client stories, donor information, internal financials. Check your organisation's policy on using online tools before you upload a deck. Also remove anything you wouldn't want shared publicly, since the finished video can travel anywhere.

## Key takeaways

- Explainer videos let a small team explain the same things consistently, at scale.
- Make one video per audience, with one clear ask.
- Follow situation, problem, answer, proof, next step.
- Write notes in warm, spoken language.
- Use only accurate, consented stories and numbers.
