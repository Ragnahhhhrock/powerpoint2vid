When you upload a presentation to YouTube, the video itself is only half the job. The title, description, chapters and thumbnail decide whether anyone finds it and whether they stay. The good news: a slide-based video gives you most of what you need. Your headlines become chapter titles, and your speaker notes become the description.

This guide walks through preparing the file, uploading it and writing the details that help people find and use it.

## Before you upload: prepare the file

### Check the format

YouTube accepts a wide range of video formats, including MP4 and WebM. If your tool exports WebM, you can usually upload it as is. If you need MP4 for other platforms, common conversion tools can handle it.

### Watch it once, all the way through

Look for:

- Slides that change before the narration finishes.
- Typos in headlines or captions.
- Any internal information that shouldn't be public.
- Audio levels that are consistent from start to end.

### Note your slide timestamps

As you watch, write down the time each new section starts. You'll use these for chapters. If your deck has section divider slides, start there.

## How to upload a presentation to YouTube

The upload process is straightforward and YouTube changes its interface from time to time, so here are the general steps rather than exact menus:

1. Sign in to YouTube and open YouTube Studio.
2. Look for the Create or Upload option and select your video file.
3. While it uploads, fill in the title, description and thumbnail.
4. Set the audience setting (whether the video is made for children).
5. Choose visibility: public, unlisted or private.
6. Publish or schedule.

**Unlisted** is useful for videos you want to share by link only, such as client walkthroughs or internal briefings. **Public** is for videos you want people to discover in search.

## Writing a title people search for

A good title says what the viewer will learn, in the words they would type.

| Weak title | Stronger title |
|---|---|
| Q4 Webinar | How to Plan a Q4 Marketing Budget for a Small Business |
| Product Overview v3 | How Ledgerly Sends Invoice Reminders Automatically |
| Lecture 7 | Supply and Demand Explained: Lecture 7, Intro to Economics |

Tips:

- Put the main topic near the start.
- Keep it readable. Aim for a title that won't be cut off on a phone.
- Avoid all caps and vague teasers.

## Writing a useful description

The description helps viewers decide whether to watch, and helps YouTube understand the topic. Structure it like this:

1. **First two lines.** A plain summary of what the video covers. These show before "more", so make them count.
2. **What you'll learn.** Three to five bullets.
3. **Chapters.** Timestamps and titles (see below).
4. **Links.** Your website, related videos, a sign-up page.

### Sample description

> Learn how automatic invoice reminders work and how to set your first one up in under five minutes.
>
> In this video:
> - Why late invoices happen
> - How reminder schedules work
> - Setting up your first reminder
>
> 0:00 Why invoices go unpaid
> 0:25 How reminders work
> 1:05 Setting up your first schedule
> 1:40 Next steps
>
> Start here: ledgerly dot com

Your speaker notes are a great source for the summary. They already explain each slide in plain words.

## Adding chapters from your slides

Chapters split the video into labelled sections on the progress bar. They help viewers jump to the part they need, and they make a long presentation feel manageable.

To add chapters, list timestamps in the description:

- The first timestamp must be 0:00.
- Each line starts with a timestamp, followed by a title.
- YouTube has minimums for the number of chapters and their length, so check its help pages if your chapters don't appear.

Slide decks make this easy. Use section divider slides or key headlines as chapter titles. If your headlines make a point, such as "Reminders go out on your schedule", they make good chapter titles too. Our post on [slide headlines that make a point](/blog/slide-headlines-that-make-a-point) shows how to write them.

## Choosing a thumbnail

The thumbnail is often the deciding factor in whether someone clicks. For a presentation video:

- Use your title slide or a strong headline slide, not a random frame.
- Keep text short and large enough to read on a phone.
- Use strong contrast.
- Make it accurate. The thumbnail should reflect what the video actually covers.

YouTube lets you pick an auto-generated frame or upload a custom image, depending on your account's features.

## Captions on YouTube

If your video has burned-in captions, they're part of the picture and always visible. YouTube also offers its own automatic captions, which viewers can turn on or off. If you have burned-in captions, you may want to check YouTube's auto captions don't double up awkwardly for your viewers.

## Quick checklist

- Video watched end to end, with no internal information visible.
- Title that names the topic in searchable words.
- Description with a two-line summary, bullets and links.
- Chapters starting at 0:00, taken from section slides.
- Readable, accurate thumbnail.
- Visibility set correctly: public, unlisted or private.
