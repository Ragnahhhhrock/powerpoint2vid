You have a .pptx file and you want a video. You don't want to learn an editor, hire a voice artist or set up a microphone. This walkthrough shows how to use a PPTX to video converter start to finish, with only a few choices to make.

If you want the long version with every option explained, read [how to convert PowerPoint to video: the complete guide](/blog/how-to-convert-powerpoint-to-video-the-complete-guide). This post is the short one.

## Before you start: two minutes of prep

A little prep saves a lot of re-exporting. Open your deck and check three things.

1. **It's a .pptx file.** Older .ppt files need to be saved as .pptx first. If your deck is in Google Slides, download it as Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx). If it's in Keynote or Canva, export it to .pptx.
2. **Your speaker notes say what you want to hear.** Your speaker notes become the narration. Empty notes mean a slide has nothing to say.
3. **Each slide has one idea.** A crowded slide is hard to read in a video because viewers can't pause and squint the way they would at a PDF.

Here's an example of notes that work well when read aloud:

> This is our three-step returns process. First, the customer fills in the online form. Second, we email a prepaid label within one business day. Third, the refund lands as soon as the parcel is scanned at our warehouse.

Short sentences. Clear order. Nothing that sounds odd when spoken.

## Step 1: Drop in the file (about 30 seconds)

Open the [PowerPoint to video converter](/) and drag your .pptx onto the page, or click to choose it.

If your deck has internal numbers, student content or client material, check your organisation's policy on using online tools before you upload it.

## Step 2: Pick a narration source (about 30 seconds)

Your speaker notes become the narration. Read them through once, as if you were speaking, and fix anything that would sound odd aloud. Slides with no notes have nothing to say, so add a sentence where you want a voice.

For most decks, this is all you need to decide.

## Step 3: Choose a voice and speed (about a minute)

Choose a voice and a speaking speed.

Try a couple of voices. Adjust the speaking speed so it sounds natural, not rushed. Slightly slower than you think usually works best for training and explainer content.

## Step 4: Let the video be designed for you (about a minute)

This is where PowerPoint2Vid differs from a plain converter. a.i. acts as director and motion designer. It reads the whole deck, tightens the story, redesigns each slide as an animated scene and times the animation to the voice. Charts are rebuilt as animated charts with the same numbers, and key points land on the word that names them.

Your job is to give it good material: a clear deck, notes that say what you mean, and one idea per slide. Ordinary slides in, extraordinary video out.

## Step 5: Check the finishing touches (about 30 seconds)

A few things round out the video:

- **Captions** help viewers who watch with the sound off, and anyone who prefers to read along.
- **Polished transitions** are part of the upgrade, so you don't need to pick them slide by slide.
- **Pacing** follows the narration, so a slide with a longer note stays up longer. Add a sentence to a note if a slide feels rushed.

## Step 6: Watch, then download

Watch the video with the voice on. Listen for words that sound wrong, slides that feel too long, and points that appear before they're mentioned. Fix them in your deck or notes and convert again.

When it's right, download it. You'll get an MP4 to share, with WebM available. WebM plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and most video editors, and converts to MP4 with common tools if a platform asks for it. Basic adds a watermark; Pro does not.

## What a PPTX to video converter like this won't do

It's worth being clear, so you can plan around it:

- It uses a synthetic voice, not a recording of your own
- It doesn't upload to YouTube or an LMS for you; you upload the file yourself

If you need your own recorded voice, PowerPoint's built-in video export can include narration and timings you record yourself. It's a fair option when that's the goal.

## Key takeaways

- Prep the deck first: .pptx format, clean notes, one idea per slide.
- Clear notes make the best narration.
- Test two or three voices and slow the speed slightly.
- Give the director good material: one idea per slide.
- Watch with sound before sharing, every time.
