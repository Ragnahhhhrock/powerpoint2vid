Most slide headlines are labels: "Overview", "Pricing", "Results", "Next steps". They tell the reader what the slide is about but not what it's saying. Good slide headline writing turns each title into a short statement of the point. For anyone who sends decks to prospects or clients, it's the single change that makes a deck readable without you there to present it.

## Why labels don't work

A label makes the audience do the work. They see "Q3 results", look at the chart and try to figure out what matters. Different people draw different conclusions. Some draw none.

A headline that makes a point tells them what to see. "Q3 revenue beat target by a comfortable margin" gives the chart a job: proving the headline.

This matters most when the deck travels without you. A prospect skimming your proposal on their phone reads headlines and little else. If the headlines carry the argument, they get the argument.

## Slide headline writing: the basic method

### 1. Ask "so what?"

Look at the slide and ask what the audience should conclude. That answer is your headline.

### 2. Write a full sentence

Include a subject and a verb. "Onboarding time" is a label. "Onboarding now takes two days instead of five" is a point.

### 3. Keep it short

Aim for one line, two at most. Cut filler words like "overview of", "update on" and "information about".

### 4. Be specific

Specific numbers, names and outcomes are more convincing than general claims. Only use figures you can back up.

### 5. Read the headlines in order

Put all your headlines in a list. They should tell the whole story without the slides. If one doesn't add anything, cut the slide or merge it.

## Before and after examples

Here's a typical sales deck, rewritten.

| Before (label) | After (point) |
|---|---|
| About us | We help mid-sized retailers cut stock write-offs |
| The challenge | Overstock ties up cash you could spend on growth |
| Our solution | Weekly demand forecasts tell you what to reorder and when |
| How it works | You connect your sales data and get a forecast in a week |
| Case study | A regional homewares chain reduced overstock in its first quarter |
| Pricing | One flat monthly fee, scaled to your number of stores |
| Next steps | A two-week pilot with three of your stores |

Read the right-hand column on its own. It's a complete pitch.

## Headlines for different kinds of slides

### Data slides

State what the data shows, not what it measures.

- Label: "Customer satisfaction scores"
- Point: "Satisfaction rose after we added weekend support"

Then design the chart to prove it: highlight the relevant bar or line and grey out the rest.

### Process slides

Say what the process achieves.

- Label: "Implementation process"
- Point: "Implementation takes four steps and about three weeks"

### Comparison slides

Say which option wins and why.

- Label: "Option A vs Option B"
- Point: "Option B costs more upfront but less over three years"

### Title and section slides

These can stay short, but they can still make a promise.

- Label: "Proposal"
- Point: "A faster way to forecast stock for Smith & Co"

## Common mistakes

- **Headlines that are too long.** If it runs to three lines, move detail into the body or notes.
- **Vague verbs.** "Improved", "enhanced" and "optimised" without a measure don't say much. Say what changed.
- **Claims the slide doesn't support.** The headline makes a promise; the body has to keep it.
- **Questions as headlines everywhere.** One question to open a section can work. A whole deck of them feels like a quiz.
- **Jargon the reader doesn't share.** Internal project names and acronyms mean nothing to a prospect. Use the words your client uses to describe their own problem.

## Headlines and speaker notes

Once the headline makes the point, the speaker notes explain it. When a deck becomes a narrated video, the notes become the voiceover. A strong pattern is to open the narration by restating the headline in spoken form, then add the detail.

*Headline:* "Implementation takes four steps and about three weeks"

*Notes:* "Getting started takes about three weeks, in four steps. First, we connect your sales data. Second, we check it with your team. Third, we run a trial forecast. And fourth, you start ordering from it."

That gives viewers the point twice: once on screen, once in their ears. ## Quick checklist

- Every headline is a full sentence with a verb
- Each one states what the audience should conclude
- One or two lines at most
- Numbers and outcomes are specific and accurate
- The slide body supports the headline
- The headlines alone tell the story in order
