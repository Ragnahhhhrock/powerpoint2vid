Most problems in a presentation video are visible in the deck before you ever press export. A typo on slide 9, a speaker note that reads a URL letter by letter, a chart nobody can read at laptop size. This PowerPoint to video checklist walks through the checks worth doing first, so the first video you record is the one you keep.

Work through it in order. It takes about ten minutes for a typical 15-slide deck, and it saves you recording the whole thing twice.

## Why a PowerPoint to video checklist saves time

A live audience forgives a stray typo. A video plays the same mistake to every viewer, every time, and fixing it later means exporting again.

A checklist moves the fixing to the cheap part of the process: editing a slide takes seconds, while re-exporting and re-checking a whole video takes far longer. If you are new to the process, the [complete guide to converting PowerPoint to video](/blog/how-to-convert-powerpoint-to-video-the-complete-guide) covers the full workflow; this post focuses on the final pass.

## 1. Check the file itself

- **Save as .pptx.** Older .ppt files need saving in the newer format first. If your deck lives in Google Slides, download it as Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx). Keynote and Canva decks need exporting to .pptx.
- **Remove hidden and spare slides.** Backup slides, "questions?" slides and old versions tucked at the end will all end up in the video.
- **Check the slide size.** 16:9 suits nearly every screen and video platform. A 4:3 deck will show bars at the sides.
- **Use common fonts.** A font that is not widely available may be swapped for a substitute. Stick with common fonts or check the preview carefully.

## 2. Check every slide on screen

Open the deck at the size most people will watch it: a laptop, not a projector. Then read each slide as if you were seeing it for the first time.

### Text and readability
- Body text at 24pt or larger. Anything smaller is hard to read in a video player.
- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two headlines, split it.
- Headlines that make a point, not labels.

Before: "Q3 results"
After: "Q3 sign-ups rose after we shortened the form"

### Contrast and colour
- Aim for WCAG AA contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text.
- Never rely on colour alone. Add labels, icons or patterns so the message survives for viewers with colour vision deficiency.

### Charts and images
- One message per chart. Highlight the bar or line that matters and grey out the rest.
- Replace blurry screenshots. Small details that were fine in a meeting room get soft when scaled for video.

## 3. Check the speaker notes (your script)

In PowerPoint2Vid, the speaker notes become the narration. So the notes deserve the same care as the slides.

- **Every slide has notes**, so nothing is left for the narration to guess at.
- **Short sentences.** One idea each. Spoken narration is far easier to follow in short sentences than in long, nested ones.
- **Numbers written the way people say them.** "About two and a half thousand" reads better than "2,487".
- **No URLs, file paths or stage directions** like "click here" or "[pause]". The voice will read them aloud.
- **Acronyms checked.** Spell out anything the voice might mispronounce, or write it phonetically.

Here is a sample speaker note before and after:

Before: "As per Fig. 3 (see appendix), FY25 NPS improved approx. 8pts QoQ - pls refer to dashboard at intranet/reports."

After: "Customer satisfaction kept climbing this year. The score rose each quarter. The full report is on the intranet."

For more on writing notes that sound natural, see [how to write speaker notes that become a great voiceover](/blog/how-to-write-speaker-notes-that-become-a-great-voiceover).

## 4. Check the settings before you export

These settings shape the finished video. Decide them once, then preview.

| Setting | What to check |
|---|---|
| Narration script | Speaker notes are the narration, so check they are complete and current |
| Voice and speed | Pick a voice that suits your audience; adjust speaking speed to match |
| Pacing | Long enough on each slide to read slides that have little narration |
| Captions | On, if your viewers may watch with the sound off |

One setting deserves a closer look:

- **Captions.** Captions appear sentence by sentence in the video. They help people watching with the sound off and viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing. If your platform needs a separate caption file such as SRT or VTT, check that before you publish.

## 5. Preview, then export

Watch the video from start to finish with the sound on. Listen for mispronounced words, rushed slides and awkward pauses. Fix them in the deck and run it again.

When it sounds right, export and download the file:

1. Play it back once, start to finish, before you share it.
2. Check that captions, numbers and names are right.
3. Check that the animation lands on the point each narration line is making.

Output is an MP4 file, with WebM available. MP4 plays almost everywhere, and WebM works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and most editors.

## Quick checklist

- File is .pptx, 16:9, with no spare slides
- Every slide has one idea and a headline that makes a point
- Body text 24pt+, contrast meets WCAG AA
- Charts have one highlighted message
- Speaker notes are short, spoken sentences with no URLs or stage directions
- Voice and speaking speed chosen
- Captions decided
- Your organisation's policy on using online tools with this deck checked
- Full video watched with sound on before you share it
