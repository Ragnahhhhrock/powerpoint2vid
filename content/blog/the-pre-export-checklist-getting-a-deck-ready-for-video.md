Most problems in a presentation video are visible in the deck before you ever press export. A typo on slide 9, a speaker note that reads a URL letter by letter, a chart nobody can read at laptop size. This PowerPoint to video checklist walks through the checks worth doing first, so the first video you record is the one you keep.

Work through it in order. It takes about ten minutes for a typical 15-slide deck, and it saves you recording the whole thing twice.

## Why a PowerPoint to video checklist saves time

Video export runs in real time. A five-minute video takes about five minutes to record, and any mistake means doing it again. A live audience forgives a stray typo. A video plays the same mistake to every viewer, every time.

A checklist moves the fixing to the cheap part of the process: editing a slide takes seconds, re-recording takes minutes. If you are new to the process, the [complete guide to converting PowerPoint to video](/blog/how-to-convert-powerpoint-to-video-the-complete-guide) covers the full workflow; this post focuses on the final pass.

## 1. Check the file itself

- **Save as .pptx.** Older .ppt files need saving in the newer format first. If your deck lives in Google Slides, download it as Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx). Keynote and Canva decks need exporting to .pptx.
- **Remove hidden and spare slides.** Backup slides, "questions?" slides and old versions tucked at the end will all end up in the video.
- **Check the slide size.** 16:9 suits nearly every screen and video platform. A 4:3 deck will show bars at the sides.
- **Use common fonts.** If a font is not installed on the device that renders the video, you may see a substitute. Stick with widely available fonts or check the preview carefully.

## 2. Check every slide on screen

Open the deck at the size most people will watch it: a laptop, not a projector. Then read each slide as if you were seeing it for the first time.

### Text and readability
- Body text at 24pt or larger. Anything smaller is hard to read in a video player.
- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two headlines, split it.
- Headlines that make a point, not labels.

Before: "Q3 results"
After: "Q3 sign-ups rose after we shortened the form"

### Contrast and colour
- Aim for WCAG AA contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text.
- Never rely on colour alone. Add labels, icons or patterns so the message survives for viewers with colour vision deficiency.

### Charts and images
- One message per chart. Highlight the bar or line that matters and grey out the rest.
- Replace blurry screenshots. Small details that were fine in a meeting room get soft when scaled for video.

## 3. Check the speaker notes (your script)

In PowerPoint2Vid, the speaker notes become the narration, read by your browser's built-in text-to-speech voice. By default it reads the speaker notes, and falls back to the slide text where a slide has no notes. So the notes deserve the same care as the slides.

- **Every slide has notes**, or you are happy for the slide text to be read instead.
- **Short sentences.** One idea each. Text-to-speech handles short sentences far better than long, nested ones.
- **Numbers written the way people say them.** "About two and a half thousand" reads better than "2,487".
- **No URLs, file paths or stage directions** like "click here" or "[pause]". The voice will read them aloud.
- **Acronyms checked.** Spell out anything the voice might mispronounce, or write it phonetically.

Here is a sample speaker note before and after:

Before: "As per Fig. 3 (see appendix), FY25 NPS improved approx. 8pts QoQ - pls refer to dashboard at intranet/reports."

After: "Customer satisfaction kept climbing this year. The score rose each quarter. The full report is on the intranet."

For more on writing notes that sound natural, see [how to write speaker notes that become a great voiceover](/blog/how-to-write-speaker-notes-that-become-a-great-voiceover).

## 4. Check the settings before you export

These settings shape the finished video. Decide them once, then preview.

| Setting | What to check |
|---|---|
| Narration script | Speaker notes, else slide text (default); notes only; slide text only; or no narration |
| Voice and speed | Pick a voice available on this device; adjust speaking speed to suit your audience |
| Minimum seconds per slide | Long enough to read slides that have little or no narration |
| Video style | Clean, Minimal cinematic, Keynote launch, UI springs, Kinetic poster or Paper & ink |
| Transition | Crossfade, slide, zoom or none |
| Options | Animate shapes one by one, burn in captions, gentle zoom on images, record narration into the video |

Two settings deserve a closer look:

- **Captions.** Burned-in captions appear sentence by sentence inside the video. They help people watching with the sound off and viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing. There is no separate caption file, so if your platform needs SRT or VTT, plan to create that separately.
- **Recording narration.** To include the voice in the video, tick the option to record narration. In Chrome, choose "this tab" and tick "share tab audio" when prompted.

## 5. Preview, then export

Watch the preview with the voice on, from start to finish. Listen for mispronounced words, rushed slides and awkward pauses. Fix them in the deck, reload and preview again.

When it sounds right, export:

1. Keep the tab in the foreground. The browser records in real time.
2. Close other heavy tabs and apps so the recording stays smooth.
3. Leave the computer alone until the recording finishes.
4. Play the file back once before you share it.

Output is usually WebM, or MP4 in browsers that support it. WebM plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and most editors, and converts to MP4 with common tools if your platform asks for it.

## Quick checklist

- File is .pptx, 16:9, with no spare slides
- Every slide has one idea and a headline that makes a point
- Body text 24pt+, contrast meets WCAG AA
- Charts have one highlighted message
- Speaker notes are short, spoken sentences with no URLs or stage directions
- Narration script, voice and speed chosen
- Minimum seconds per slide set
- Style, transition and captions chosen
- Tab audio shared if recording narration
- Full preview watched with sound on
- Tab kept in the foreground during export
