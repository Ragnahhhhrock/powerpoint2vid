You send your deck to an investor, a partner or an accelerator, and then you wait. Did they open it? Did they get past slide four? Did they understand your traction chart without you there to explain it? A pitch deck video solves part of that problem. It lets your deck explain itself, in your order, with the emphasis you would give it in the room.

This playbook walks through turning the deck you already have into a short narrated video you can send, embed or attach to an application.

## Why a pitch deck video helps

A static PDF deck relies on the reader to work out your story. Many will skim. A narrated video does three things a PDF cannot:

- **It controls the order and pace.** Viewers see the problem before the solution, and the traction before the ask.
- **It explains the hard slides.** Market size, unit economics and competitive positioning often need a sentence of context.
- **It travels well.** One link works in an email, an application form, a website or a follow-up after a meeting.

It does not replace a live conversation. It gets you to one with a reader who already understands the basics.

## Step 1: Tighten the deck before you narrate it

A video exposes weak slides quickly, so trim first. A typical pitch story looks like this:

1. **Problem.** Who has it and why it hurts.
2. **Solution.** What you do, in one sentence.
3. **Product.** A simple visual or screenshot.
4. **Market.** Who buys and how many of them there are.
5. **Business model.** How you make money.
6. **Traction.** Evidence that it is working.
7. **Competition.** Where you fit.
8. **Team.** Why you.
9. **Ask.** What you want and what it will achieve.

For the video version, aim for one idea per slide and a headline that makes the point.

Before: "Traction"
After: "Paying customers have grown every month since launch"

Move detail into the speaker notes or an appendix you share separately.

## Step 2: Write the script in your speaker notes

Your speaker notes become the narration, read aloud as the narration. Write each note as two to four short spoken sentences.

Sample notes for three slides:

**Problem:** "Small clinics still book most appointments by phone. Staff spend hours a week on calls. Patients wait on hold, then give up."

**Solution:** "We give clinics a booking page that fills their calendar automatically. Patients book in under a minute. Staff get their phone time back."

**Ask:** "We're raising our first round to grow our sales team. It will take us into two new states this year. We'd love to talk."

Tips for founder scripts:

- Lead with the benefit, then the detail.
- Use real numbers from your own data, said the way people say them.
- Avoid jargon an investor outside your industry would trip on.
- Keep the ask specific and simple.

## Step 3: Choose a look that fits your brand

Pick a direction that matches your brand and audience.

| Direction | Feel | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Polished and modern | Sleek, confident, product-led | Product and tech startups wanting a launch feel |
| Calm and premium | Slow, understated, spacious | Considered, high-end brands |
| Bold and energetic | Punchy, fast-moving | A short teaser or consumer brand |

The [guide to choosing a video style](/blog/how-to-choose-a-video-style-for-your-slide-deck) covers how to match the look and feel to your audience.

Include captions so the video still works with the sound off.

## Step 4: Preview, refine and export

1. Drop your .pptx into PowerPoint2Vid. If your deck is in Google Slides, download it as .pptx first; Keynote and Canva decks need exporting to .pptx.
2. Choose a voice and speaking speed. Pick one that sounds clear and confident at a moderate pace.
3. Make sure dense slides like the market or team slide get enough narration to stay up long enough.
4. Watch it from start to finish. Fix any mispronounced product names by rewriting them phonetically in the notes.
5. Download the video.

If the deck contains unreleased financials or product plans, check your organisation's policy on using online tools before uploading.

## Step 5: Share it where it gets watched

- **Investor emails.** Upload the video to a host you already use and link to it with one line of context.
- **Application forms.** Many accelerator and grant forms ask for a short video. Check length and format limits.
- **Your website.** A short version can sit on an "investors" or "about" page.
- **After meetings.** Send it as a recap so the partner can share it internally.

The video downloads as an MP4, with WebM also available, so it suits most forms. For more on async pitching, see [sales teams: send a narrated pitch deck, not a cold PDF](/blog/sales-teams-send-a-narrated-pitch-deck-not-a-cold-pdf).

## Quick checklist

- Deck trimmed to one idea per slide
- Headlines that make a point
- Notes written as short spoken sentences
- Product and company names checked for pronunciation
- Look and feel chosen to match your brand
- Captions burned in for silent viewing
- Full preview watched with sound on
- File format checked for where you will share it
