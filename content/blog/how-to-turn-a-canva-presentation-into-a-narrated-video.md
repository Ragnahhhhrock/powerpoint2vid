Canva makes good-looking slides quickly, which is why so many marketers and founders build their decks there. But a deck on its own doesn't explain itself. If you want a Canva presentation to video with a voiceover that walks people through it, the most flexible route is to export the design as a PowerPoint file and convert that. This post covers the export, the clean-up most Canva decks need, and how to add narration without recording your own voice.

## Your options for a Canva presentation to video

There are a few ways to get from Canva slides to a video. Each suits a different job.

- **Download from Canva as a video.** Canva can export designs in video formats. This works well for silent, animated social clips where you've set the timing yourself in Canva.
- **Record yourself presenting.** Canva has presentation and recording features if you want to talk over the slides on camera or with your own microphone.
- **Export as .pptx and convert with narration.** Download the presentation as a PowerPoint file, write your script in the speaker notes, and turn it into a narrated video with a text-to-speech voice.

This post focuses on the third route. It's the one to choose when you want narration but don't want to record your own voice, when you'll need to update the video later, or when you want a consistent voice across a series.

## Step 1: Export your Canva design as PowerPoint

In Canva, open your presentation and look for the Share or Download option. Choose the Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx) format and download the file.

A few things to know before you do:

- Canva converts its designs into PowerPoint shapes, text boxes and images. Most layouts come across well, but some effects may not translate exactly.
- Canva-only fonts may not be available outside Canva. They might be swapped for a similar font.
- Some Canva animations and video elements may not come across, or may become still images.

That's normal for any export between tools. The next step is to check and tidy.

## Step 2: Check the exported deck

Open the .pptx in PowerPoint, or upload it to Google Slides, and flick through every slide. Look for:

### Fonts

If a heading font has changed, either pick a close substitute available on your computer or use a standard font that comes with PowerPoint. Check that headlines still fit on one or two lines.

### Text boxes

Text sometimes shifts slightly or wraps differently. Fix any lines that now overlap an image or run off the slide.

### Images and backgrounds

Make sure background images and photos are still in place and not stretched. Check that text over images still has strong contrast.

### Grouped elements

Canva designs often use many small elements. If something looks out of place, it may have been grouped differently. Ungroup, nudge, and regroup as needed.

Ten minutes of checking here saves you from discovering a broken slide halfway through your video preview.

## Step 3: Write the narration in the speaker notes

The speaker notes are where the voiceover comes from. Each slide's notes are read aloud while that slide is on screen.

Canva has its own notes feature, and notes may or may not come across in the export depending on your setup. Check each slide. If they're missing or rough, write them fresh in PowerPoint or Google Slides.

### How to write good notes

- Write as you'd speak. Short sentences. Contractions are fine.
- Say the point of the slide in the first sentence.
- Describe anything important on the slide in words, so the narration makes sense on its own.
- Keep each slide's notes to a few sentences. Long notes usually mean the slide should be split.

### A sample

Here's a launch deck slide from a small skincare brand.

Slide headline: "Made in small batches in Fremantle"

Speaker notes:

> "Every jar is made in small batches at our workshop in Fremantle. That means fresher stock, and it means we can test every batch before it ships. If something's not right, it doesn't leave the building."

Three sentences. One point. It sounds like a person talking, not a brochure.

For more on this, see [how to write speaker notes that become a great voiceover](/blog/how-to-write-speaker-notes-that-become-a-great-voiceover).

## Step 4: Convert the .pptx to a narrated video

Now drop the .pptx into a converter that reads speaker notes aloud. With PowerPoint2Vid, you'll:

1. **Load the file.** It's parsed in your browser and never uploaded.
2. **Choose the narration script.** "Speaker notes, else slide text" is the default. "Speaker notes only" is a good choice when you've written notes for every slide.
3. **Pick a voice and speaking speed.** Available voices depend on your device and browser, so try a couple.
4. **Choose a video style.** Canva decks are often bold and colourful. Kinetic poster suits punchy launch content; Minimal cinematic or Clean suit calmer brand stories. See [how to choose a video style for your slide deck](/blog/how-to-choose-a-video-style-for-your-slide-deck) for more.
5. **Set options.** Turn on captions if the video will autoplay without sound. Pick a transition: crossfade, slide, zoom or none.
6. **Preview with voice.** Watch it through and note anything to fix.
7. **Export.** The browser records the video in real time, so keep the tab in the foreground. You'll usually get WebM, or MP4 in browsers that support it.

## Step 5: Fix and re-export

After your first preview, you'll probably want to tweak something: a word the voice mispronounces, a slide that feels rushed, a headline that's too long. Make the change in the .pptx, load it again and preview. Because the voice comes from your notes, there's nothing to re-record.

If you change the design itself, you can edit in Canva and export a fresh .pptx, or make small fixes directly in PowerPoint. Pick one source of truth so you don't end up with two versions drifting apart.

## Quick checklist

- Download the Canva design as Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx).
- Check fonts, text boxes, images and contrast on every slide.
- Write or tidy the speaker notes: short, spoken, one point per slide.
- Choose a voice, speed, style and transition that suit the brand.
- Turn on captions if people will watch with sound off.
- Preview, fix, then export with the tab in the foreground.
