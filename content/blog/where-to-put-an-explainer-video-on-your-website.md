You have made the video. Now the question is where it goes. An explainer video on website pages only helps if the right people see it at the moment they have the question it answers. Drop it in the wrong place and it becomes decoration. Put it in the right place and it does a sales or support job every day without you.

This guide covers the placements that tend to work, how to match the video to the page, and the small details that decide whether anyone presses play.

## Start with the question, not the page

Every explainer video answers one question. Before you choose a spot, write that question down.

- "What does this company actually do?"
- "How does this feature work?"
- "Is the paid plan worth it?"
- "How do I get started?"

Then ask where a visitor is when they have that question. That is where the video goes. One video per question, one question per video, keeps everything easy to place.

If you are still deciding what kind of video you need, [What Is an Explainer Video? A Plain-English Guide](/blog/what-is-an-explainer-video-a-plain-english-guide) covers the basics.

## The best spots for an explainer video on website pages

### 1. The home page, near the top

The home page gets visitors who know the least about you. A short overview video answers "what is this and is it for me?" Put it beside or just under your main headline, not at the bottom of the page where few people scroll.

Keep this one short. It should explain the problem you solve and the result people get, then hand over to the page.

### 2. Product or feature pages

Feature pages attract people who are already interested and want detail. A walkthrough video here can show the steps that are hard to describe in text. Place it next to the feature description, so the video and the words support each other.

### 3. The pricing page

Pricing pages are where people hesitate. A short video that explains what each plan includes, or who each plan suits, can answer doubts before they become a reason to leave. Keep it calm and factual. This is not the place for a hard sell.

### 4. Landing pages for campaigns

If you run ads or email campaigns, the landing page should match the promise in the ad. A video made from the same deck as the campaign keeps the message consistent from click to sign-up.

### 5. Help centre and onboarding pages

Support content is where explainer videos earn their keep over time. The same question comes in again and again, and a video answers it the same way every time. Place each video at the top of the matching help article, with the written steps underneath for people who prefer to read.

### 6. Inside emails that link back to the site

Most email tools do not play video inline, but a thumbnail image linking to the page with the video works well. The email does the nudging; the page does the explaining.

## Matching the video to the page

Different pages need different videos. A quick guide:

| Page | Visitor's question | Video length | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | What is this? | Short | Broad and clear |
| Feature | How does it work? | Short to medium | Step by step |
| Pricing | Which plan suits me? | Short | Factual, reassuring |
| Campaign landing | Is this what the ad promised? | Short | Matches the ad |
| Help centre | How do I do this? | As long as the task needs | Instructional |

You do not need five separate productions for this. One master deck can feed several videos. Duplicate it, cut it down for each page, and adjust the speaker notes for the audience.

## Details that decide whether people press play

### Write a clear label

Put a short line above or beside the player that says what the video covers and how long it is. For example: "See how it works in 2 minutes". People are more likely to start a video when they know what they will get.

### Choose a strong first frame

The first slide doubles as the thumbnail in many players. Make it a title slide with a clear headline, not a logo on a blank background.

Before: "Welcome"
After: "Turn your deck into a narrated video"

### Plan for silent viewing

Many visitors browse with the sound off. Burned-in captions let them follow the narration without audio, which also helps people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

### Keep the page fast

Large video files can slow a page down. Hosting the video on a video platform and embedding it, or using a player that loads only when someone clicks, keeps the page quick. Check your page speed after adding the video, not just before.

### Do not autoplay with sound

Video that starts playing loudly on page load annoys people. If you autoplay at all, keep it muted and make sure the video still makes sense with captions.

## Keeping videos current

Websites change. Products change faster. An out-of-date video is worse than none, because it tells visitors something that is no longer true.

The simplest fix is to make videos from slides you control. When a feature changes, edit the slide and the speaker notes, then export the video again. There is no reshoot and no voice session to book.

## Quick checklist

- Write down the single question each video answers
- Place the video where visitors have that question
- Put home and pricing videos high on the page
- Add a short label with the topic and length
- Use a title slide with a clear headline as the first frame
- Burn in captions for silent viewers
- Embed in a way that keeps the page fast
- Review videos whenever the product or pricing changes
