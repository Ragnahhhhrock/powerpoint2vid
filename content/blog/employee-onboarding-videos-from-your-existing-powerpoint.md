Every HR or L&D team has an onboarding deck. Usually several. They cover the company story, policies, systems, safety and who to ask for what. Someone presents them live to each new starter, again and again. Making an onboarding video from PowerPoint decks you already have turns that repeated session into something new hires can watch on day one, at their own pace, and get the same accurate message every time.

This playbook walks through the process, from auditing your existing decks to keeping the videos current when things change.

## Why turn onboarding decks into video

Live onboarding sessions have real value. People meet their colleagues and ask questions. But much of the content is the same every time: the leave policy, how to log a support ticket, the code of conduct.

Moving that standard content into video helps in a few ways:

- **Consistency.** Every new hire hears the same information, explained the same way.
- **Timing.** People can start before the next scheduled session, or rewatch a section later.
- **Time back for trainers.** Live sessions can focus on questions, introductions and discussion.
- **Easy updates.** When a policy changes, you edit the slide and notes, then export again.

## Step 1: Audit your existing decks

Gather every deck used in onboarding. Then sort the content into three groups.

1. **Stable and standard.** Company history, values, core policies, how systems work. This is ideal for video.
2. **Changes often.** Org charts, current projects, named contacts. Keep these out of the video, or put them on a single slide that's easy to swap.
3. **Needs a human.** Welcome from a manager, team introductions, Q&A. Keep these live.

Aim for a set of short videos rather than one long one. A new hire is more likely to finish five six-minute videos than one thirty-minute video. A sensible split might look like this:

- Welcome and how we work
- Policies you need to know
- Systems and tools
- Health, safety and wellbeing
- Where to get help

## Step 2: Clean up the slides

Onboarding decks tend to collect clutter over the years. Before converting, tidy each one.

- **One idea per slide.** Split slides that cover several policies.
- **Headlines that say something.** "You can work from home up to three days a week" beats "Flexible work policy".
- **Large text.** 24pt or larger for body text.
- **Remove names and dates that will go stale.** Use roles instead: "your manager", "the IT service desk".
- **Check your brand template.** A consistent template makes the video series feel like a set.

## Step 3: Write the narration in the speaker notes

This is where most of the work happens, and where you get the most value. The speaker notes become the voiceover, so write them as you'd explain things to a new colleague.

**Sample slide:** "Log IT problems through the service portal, not by email"

**Sample speaker notes:**

> If something on your laptop isn't working, log it through the service portal. Don't email the IT team directly. The portal makes sure your request goes to the right person, and you can track its progress. You'll find the link on the intranet home page. If you can't log in at all, call the service desk number on the back of your ID card.

Keep sentences short. Spell out acronyms the first time, because a new starter won't know them yet. Write numbers the way you'd say them.

## Step 4: Convert the deck into an onboarding video from PowerPoint

Open PowerPoint2Vid in your browser and drop in the .pptx. The file is processed on your own device, and slides are never uploaded. That matters for internal content like policies, systems screenshots and organisational information.

Then set it up:

- **Narration.** Use speaker notes as the script. The voice comes from your browser's built-in text-to-speech voices, so the options depend on your device and browser. Pick one that sounds clear and set a comfortable speaking speed.
- **Style.** For onboarding, Clean or UI springs tend to feel friendly and professional. Keep the same style across the whole series.
- **Transitions.** Crossfade or slide work well and don't distract.
- **Captions.** Turn on burned-in captions. They help people watching without sound and those reading in a second language.
- **Minimum seconds per slide.** Set this so short slides don't rush past.

Preview with the voice before exporting. Listen for product names, internal acronyms or place names the voice mispronounces, and respell them in the notes so they sound right.

## Step 5: Export and share

Exporting records the video in real time in your browser, so keep the tab in the foreground until it finishes. In Chrome, choose "this tab" and tick "share tab audio" to record the narration into the file. The output is usually WebM, or MP4 where the browser supports it.

Then upload the videos to wherever new hires already look: your LMS, intranet or HR platform. Add each video to the onboarding checklist with a short description of what it covers.

## Step 6: Keep the videos current

The biggest advantage of slide-based video is how easy it is to update. When a policy changes:

1. Edit the relevant slide and its speaker notes.
2. Reload the deck and preview the changed section.
3. Export again and replace the old file.

No re-recording. No booking voice talent. No re-shooting. Set a review date for each video, perhaps every six months, and assign an owner.

## Quick checklist

- Decks audited and split into short topics
- Stale names, dates and contacts removed
- Headlines rewritten to make a point
- Speaker notes written as spoken explanations
- Acronyms spelled out and checked for pronunciation
- Same style and voice across the series
- Captions turned on
- Owner and review date set for each video
