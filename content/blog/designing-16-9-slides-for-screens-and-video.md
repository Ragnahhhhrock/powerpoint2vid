Most presentations are now watched on laptops, monitors, phones and video players, not projected on a wall. Almost all of those screens are wide. Good 16:9 slide design uses that wide frame on purpose, rather than stretching a layout built for an old square-ish projector. It also leaves room for the things video adds, such as captions.

This post covers why 16:9 matters, how to set it up, and layouts that make the most of the width.

## Why 16:9 is the default

16:9 is the shape of most laptop screens, TVs and online video players. A 16:9 slide fills those screens edge to edge. A 4:3 slide shown on the same screen gets black or white bars down each side, which wastes space and looks dated.

When you turn a deck into a video, the case is even stronger. Video players and platforms are built around 16:9, so a wide deck becomes a full-frame video without letterboxing.

## Setting up for 16:9 slide design

PowerPoint, Google Slides and Keynote all default to 16:9 for new presentations. Older decks and some corporate templates may still be 4:3.

To check, look at the slide size or page setup option in your tool. If it says 4:3 or "Standard", you have an older format.

### Converting an old 4:3 deck

Changing the size of an existing deck usually prompts a choice to scale content up or keep it the same size. Neither is perfect. Expect to:

- Move content out from the centre to use the extra width.
- Resize or re-crop images that now look small.
- Check that nothing has been stretched or distorted.
- Update the slide master so new slides follow the wide layout.

It is often quicker to fix the master and layouts first, then go through slides one by one. For more on masters, see [Slide Masters and Templates: Set Up Once, Save Hours](/blog/slide-masters-and-templates-set-up-once-save-hours).

## Leave safe margins

Content placed right at the edge feels cramped and can be cut off by some players or cropped when a video is reused elsewhere.

- Keep text and key visuals inside a margin of roughly 5 percent of the slide width on every side.
- Leave extra space at the bottom if you plan to burn in captions. Captions sit in the lower part of the frame and should not cover your content.
- Avoid placing important labels in the bottom corners, where player controls often appear.

A simple way to do this is to add guides in your slide master and keep everything inside them.

## Layouts that use the width

A wide slide is good at showing two things side by side. Lean into that.

### Split screen

Image or chart on one side, headline and a short line of text on the other. This is the most useful 16:9 layout and works for most content slides.

### Left to right flow

Processes and timelines read naturally across a wide slide. Three to five steps fit comfortably.

### Comparison

Two equal columns: before and after, option A and option B. The width gives each side room to breathe.

### Full-bleed image with a headline

A photo that fills the slide, with a short headline in a clear area. Check contrast carefully. A darkened band behind the text often helps.

## Size text for the smallest screen

A video slide might end up in a small window or on a phone. Size for that.

- Headlines around 36 to 44pt.
- Body text at 24pt or larger.
- Labels on charts and diagrams no smaller than 18pt.
- Body text left-aligned, not justified.

If text does not fit at those sizes, the slide has too much on it. Move detail into the speaker notes.

**Before (4:3 layout stretched to 16:9)**

A headline at the top. Six bullet points stacked down the middle. A small logo in each corner. Empty bands on the left and right.

**After (designed for 16:9)**

Headline across the top: "Our response time halved this year". Left half: a simple two-bar chart, last year in grey, this year in the accent colour, each directly labelled. ## How 16:9 slides look in video

PowerPoint2Vid turns your .pptx into a video, and several of its styles add motion or frame the slide in different ways. The Clean style shows your slide full-bleed. Keynote launch places the slide as a rounded card on a dark stage, and Paper & ink pins it to paper. Others add a slow push-in. Generous margins help in all of them, because a little movement or framing never crowds your content.

Preview your deck with voice in a couple of styles. If anything feels tight against the edge, pull it in.

## Quick checklist

- Slide size set to 16:9 in your presentation tool.
- Master updated with wide layouts and margin guides.
- Content inside safe margins, with room at the bottom for captions.
- Layouts that use the width: split screen, flows, comparisons.
- Text sized for a phone screen.
- Old 4:3 slides rebuilt, not just stretched.
